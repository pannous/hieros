
- Oracle 子/巳 swap: in Shang 干支 tables the child pictogram (Oracular 󵉠 U+F5260, glyph uni5DF3) fills branch 6 巳, the hair/head sign (󵈒 U+F5212, uni5B50) fills branch 1 子. Evidence is positional (full 60-day cycle tables, e.g. 合集 37986), not semantic. Seal 子 descends graphically from the child pictogram. The snake-like squiggles in the 巳 已 己 series (󵃁 = 䖵, 󵌽 = 氒, 󸍩󸍴󸍒󸍬 unidentified uF33xx) are 虫/它-type signs, not branch 巳.
- Oracle identification caution (子/學 discussion 2026-09-23): the child-with-arms (󵉠, font: 巳) and the box-head-on-legs 子 series (󵈒) have no intermediate shapes; they're joined only by the later word 子. The box-head series forms a visual continuum with the big-head-on-legs family (鬼 異 黑, unidentified 󽺖 uFA572) and survives in 儿-based signs 兄 󵄞, 克 󵄣, 兌 󵄤 (→说). 字 󵈔 is a clean child-with-arms under a roof, so the child-with-arms = "child" reading is solid. Oracle "學" = 爻 over a building; 爻 is a stable sign in every script, the 子 and hands were added in bronze; 說文 files 學 as abbreviated 斆 "teach" under 教. Rule: identifications backed by calendar position or a dated chain of shapes are solid; resemblance to a modern character alone is a guess.
- 巳 worm ≈ fetus: 說文 on 包 "象人裹妊，巳在中，象子未成形也" (巳 = the unformed child inside the womb) and on 巳 "巳為蛇，象形" (巳 = snake). Seal 包 𾨮 U+3EA2E is literally a 巳-curl inside 勹. In oracle, slot 巳 is the child-with-arms (not snake-shaped); the snake-shaped 巳 is seal-era. Compare 倮虫 "naked creature" = humans. Render: probes/fetus_snake.png
- Oracle 汜 󵍂 (U+F5342) = water + 巳, and its 巳 is a square head on a curled, armless tail: the missing link between the child-with-arms 巳 󵉠 and the head-plus-curl signs 󸍩 󸍒 (square head) and 󸍴 󸍬 (round head). So the Shang already drew 巳 as head + curl inside compounds, which supports worm ≈ fetus in the oracle layer too. Render: probes/tuo_vs_worms.png
