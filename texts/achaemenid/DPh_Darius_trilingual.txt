DPh — Trilingual Gold/Silver Foundation Inscription of Darius I
================================================================

IDENTIFICATION
Siglum: DPh (Kent/Schmitt) = "Darius, Persepolis, inscription h".
Text identical to DHa (ex-DH) from Hamadan.
Four foundation documents of the Apadana at Persepolis: two gold tablets
(DPhG) and two silver (DPhS), found in situ by F. Krefter in 1933 in the
NE and SE corner deposits, plus one gold + one silver pair from Hamadan.
Now in Tehran (four in the Ministry of Education, two in the royal palace).
Trilingual: Old Persian (10 ll.) / Elamite (7 ll.) / Akkadian (8 ll.),
with minor line-break variation between copies but identical wording.

SOURCES (cross-checked)
- Herzfeld, Altpersische Inschriften (Berlin 1938), 18f. no. 6, pl. VI
  -- editio princeps; all three languages line-by-line + synoptic text
  + German translation.
- Schmitt, Die altpersischen Inschriften der Achaimeniden (2009), 119f.
  -- modern critical edition of Old Persian: phonemic sign-level
  transliteration, normalized transcription, German translation.
  Cites Kent 1953, 109a/136b-137a; Vallat 1977, 147f.; Trümpelmann 1988,
  pl. 36-37; Schweiger 1998, I 12f., II 29-31; Schmitt 2000a, 63f. and
  pl. 32-33; Kuhrt 2007, 476f.
- English renderings cross-checked against avesta.org and livius.org
  (both Kent-derived).
- No independent modern re-edition of the Elamite/Akkadian versions
  (e.g. Grillot-Susini/Herrenschmidt for Elamite; von Voigtlander/Steve
  for Akkadian) was accessible; those two versions rest on Herzfeld 1938
  alone, checked internally against its own synoptic apparatus and
  against the parallel Old Persian.

NOTE ON CUNEIFORM RECONSTRUCTION
The Old Persian cuneiform below is a MECHANICAL RECONSTRUCTION: each
Unicode Old Persian sign (block U+103A0-103D5, verified against the
Unicode 17.0 code chart) was chosen by applying Kent's documented Old
Persian orthographic rules (dedicated Ci/Cu signs used where they exist;
plain vowel sign appended to mark long a/i/u; bare Ca-sign used for
coda/cluster consonants; XS-ideogram 𐏋 for xšayathiya) to the published
transliteration. It has NOT been checked against the tablets' photographs
(Schmitt 2000a pl. 32-33) and should be verified there before being cited
as a facsimile.
For the Akkadian and Elamite versions, the source transliterations available
here do not preserve the logogram-vs-syllabic distinction needed to know
which signs the scribes actually used (e.g. whether "sarru rabu" was written
with Sumerograms LUGAL GAL or spelled out) -- reconstructing Unicode
cuneiform for these two would risk inventing sign choices, so it is
deliberately omitted. See Herzfeld 1938 pl. VI for the original sign forms.

OLD PERSIAN
================================================================

Transliteration (Herzfeld 1938 / Kent, sign-by-sign, 10 ll., "." = word
divider):
1  daryvus . xs . vzrk . xs . xsynam . xs
2  . dhyuvnam . vistasphya . pu\xe7a
3  . hxamnisiy . θatiy . daryvu
4  s . xs . im . xsçrm . ty . adm . dary
5  amiy . hca . skibis . tyiy . pr
6  . sugdm . amt . yata . a . kusa .
7  hca . hiduv . amt . yata . a . sp
8  rda . tymiy . aurmzda . frabr
9  . hy . mθist . bganam . mam . au
10 rmzda . patuv . utamiy . viθm

Normalized transcription (Schmitt 2009):
§1  Darayava.us, xsayathiya vazrka, xsayathiya xsayathiyanam,
    xsayathiya dahyunam, Vistaspahya puça, Haxamanisiya.
§2  thati Darayava.us xsayathiya: ima xsaçam
    taya adam darayami, haca Sakaibis tayai para Sugdam
    amata yata a Kusa, haca Hindau amata yata a Sparda,
    tayamai Auramazda frabara, haya mathista baganam;
    mam Auramazda patu utamai vitham.

Literal English translation:
"Darius, the great king, king of kings, king of countries, son of
Hystaspes, an Achaemenid. Darius the king says: This is the empire
which I hold, from the Sacae who are beyond Sogdiana as far as Kush,
from Sind as far as Sardis (Lydia) -- that which Ahuramazda, the
greatest of the gods, bestowed on me. May Ahuramazda protect me and my
house."

================================================================
AKKADIAN (Babylonian)
================================================================

Transliteration (Herzfeld 1938, 8 ll.):
1  Da-a-ri-ia-wus sarru rabu-u sar sarrani sar matate apal
2  Us-ta-as-pa A-ha-ma-nis-si-' Da-a-ri-'ia-
3  wus sarru i-gab-bi: sarrutu agata sa anaku kul-
4  laka ultu gimirri sa nibirtum Suguddu
5  adi Kusu ultu Induwa adi
6  Sapardu sa Ahuramazda iddinu
7  sa rabu alla ilani Ahuramazda
8  anaku lissuranni itti ilani u ana bitija.

Literal English translation:
"Darius, great king, king of kings, king of lands, son of Hystaspes, an
Achaemenid. Darius the king says: This kingship which I hold, from all
that is beyond Sogdiana as far as Kush, from India as far as Sardis --
which Ahuramazda, who (is) greatest among the gods, gave (me); may he
protect me together with the gods and for my house."

Cuneiform: not reconstructed here -- see caveat above (logogram vs.
syllabic spelling of the royal titulary cannot be determined from the
available transliteration). See Herzfeld 1938 pl. VI.

ELAMITE
================================================================

Transliteration (Herzfeld 1938, 7 ll.):
1  Da-ri-ia-wa-u-is sunki-r sunkik sunki-p-na sunkik da-
2  ri-ia-u-is-pe-na Wi-is-ta-as-pa-na sa-ak-ri Ha-ka-man-ni-is-si-
3  ia Da-ri-ia-wa-u-is sunkik na-an-ri sunki-um-me ap-po u-
4  mar-ri-ra Sa-ak-ka-pe ik-ki-mar Ak-ka-pe Su-uk-ta mi-in-tur
5  ma Kus u-sa-ia Hi-in-du-is-mar Kus Us-par-ta ap-po
6  Uramasda u tu-un-is-ta ak-ka ir-sa-ra na-ap-pan-na
7  u Uramasda un ni-is-ke-is-ne ku-ut-ta ul-hi id-mi

Literal English translation:
"Darius, king, king of kings, king of the lands (peoples), (he is)
king; Darius, son of Hystaspes, an Achaemenid. Darius the king says:
This land which I hold -- from the Saka, who are beyond Sogdiana, to
Kush; from Sind (that is) beyond, to Kush (and) Sparda (Lydia) -- that
which Auramazda, the greatest among the gods, gave (to me). And may
Auramazda, together with the gods, protect this house(hold) for me."

Cuneiform: not reconstructed here -- see caveat above. See Herzfeld
1938 pl. VI.

Note: the Akkadian version alone explicitly adds "together with the
gods" (itti ilani) to the protection formula (§2i-j) -- noted already
by Herzfeld -- while Old Persian and Elamite phrase the final clause
more tersely. Genuine textual variant across the three versions, not a
translation error.
