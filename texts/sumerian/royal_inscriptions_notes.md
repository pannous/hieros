# Royal Inscriptions — working translations

Source: `sumtablets/` (colesimmons/SumTablets, CC-BY-4.0, 2,908 rows tagged
"Royal Inscription" across train/validation/test). IDs are CDLI P-numbers
(single artifact) or Q-numbers (composite text).

Translations below are my own working renderings from the transliteration —
**not** quotes from a published edition. For authoritative scholarly
translations, see Frayne, *Royal Inscriptions of Mesopotamia: Early Periods*
(RIME), vols. 1–4, and cross-check the ID on cdli.earth.

Most Ur III seal/dedication inscriptions (1,832 of the 2,908 rows) are minor
name/title variants of one template: *"[King], mighty man/king, king of Ur,
king of the four quarters — [official], [title], is your/his servant."*
Only a representative handful are listed here rather than all variants.

## Early Dynastic IIIb (~2500–2340 BCE)

**Q001042** — Ur-Nanše, founder of the Lagash I dynasty
```
d nin-ŋir2-su ur d nanše lugal lagaš dumu gu e2 ti-ra-aš2 mu-du3
```
For Ninŋirsu: Ur-Nanše, king of Lagash, son of Gu-..., built the E-tiraš temple.

**Q001337** — Elili, early king of Ur
```
d en-ki lugal eridu{ki}-ra e2-li-li lugal uri2{ki}-ma-ke4 abzu-ni mu-na-du3
```
For Enki, king of Eridu: Elili, king of Ur, built his Abzu (temple).

**Q001168** — Irikagina (Urukagina) era
```
d ba-u2 nam-šita iri-inim-gi-na-ka-ke4 ba-ra2 mu-bi
```
For the goddess Bau: the offering-priest(ess) of Irikagina — "bara" is its name.

## Old Akkadian (~2340–2200 BCE)

**Q000876** — Utu-hegal boundary inscription
```
d nanše nin uru16 nin in-dub-ba-ra d utu-he2-gal2 lugal an-ub-da limmu5-ba-ke4
ki-sur-ra lagaš{ki}-ka lu2 uri...
```
For Nanše, mighty lady, lady of the boundary-marker: Utu-hegal, king of the
four quarters, (fixed) the boundary territory of Lagash ...

**Q001432** — seal of an official of Šar-kali-šarri
```
lu2{d}šara2 dumu e2-da-tibir arad2 šar-ga-li2-ri2
```
Lu-Šara, son of Edatibir, servant of Šar-kali-šarri.

**Q005030** — seal naming a ruler of Kiš
```
na-bi2{d}suen ensi2 kiš{ki} šu-i3-li2-su agrig arad2-zu
```
Nabi-Suen, ruler of Kiš — Šu-ilišu the steward is your servant.

## Lagash II (~2120–2110 BCE, Gudea dynasty)

**Q000925** — Gudea, dedication to Šulšaga
```
d šul-šag4-ga dumu ki aŋ2 d nin-ŋir2-su-ka lugal-a-ni gu3-de2-a ensi2 lagaš{ki}-ke4
e2-ki-tuš-akkil-li2-ni mu-...
```
For Šulšaga, beloved son of Ninŋirsu, his master: Gudea, ruler of Lagash,
(built) his E-kitush-akkilli.

**Q000913** — Gudea, dedication to Ninŋirsu
```
d nin-ŋir2-su ur-saŋ kalag-ga d en-lil2-la2 lugal-a-ni gu3-de2-a ensi2 lagaš{ki}-ke4
niŋ2-ul-e pa mu-na-e3 ...
```
For Ninŋirsu, mighty warrior of Enlil, his master: Gudea, ruler of Lagash,
made ancient things resplendent for him, (built his temple) ...

## Ur III (~2112–2004 BCE)

**Q004126** — Amar-Suen seal formula
```
d amar{d}suen nita kalag-ga lugal uri5{ki}-ma lugal an-ub-da limmu2-ba
dan-i3-li2 dub-sar dumu arad2-zu
```
Amar-Suen, mighty man, king of Ur, king of the four quarters — Dan-ili the
scribe, son of ..., is your servant.

**Q004968** — Ibbi-Suen seal formula
```
d i-bi2{d}suen lugal kalag-ga lugal urim5{ki}-ma lugal an-ub-da limmu2-ba
lu2-diŋir-ra dumu dub-sar lugal
```
Ibbi-Suen, mighty king, king of Ur, king of the four quarters — Lu-dingira,
son of the royal scribe.

## Old Babylonian (~1900–1600 BCE)

**Q002223** — Samsu-iluna (son of Hammurabi)
```
sa-am-su-i-lu-na lugal kala-ga lugal babilim2{ki} lugal kiš{ki}-a
lugal an-ub-da limmu2-ba-ke4 u6-nir ki-tuš ma...
```
Samsu-iluna, mighty king, king of Babylon, king of Kiš, king of the four
quarters — (built) the ziggurat, the exalted dwelling ...

## Neo-Assyrian / Neo-Babylonian (1st millennium BCE, archaizing Sumerian)

**Q003850** — royal restoration at Ur
```
d nanna lugal d en-lil2-e-ne lugal-a-ni {m}{d}... šagina urim2{ki}-ma
u2-a eridug{ki}-ga e2-an-šar2 ki-tuš nam-lu...
```
For Nanna, king among the gods, his master: [royal name], governor of Ur,
provider for Eridu — (restored) the Eanšar, the dwelling ... A first-
millennium Assyrian or Babylonian king using archaizing Sumerian to
commemorate restoration work on Ur's temples (cf. Nabonidus's building
inscriptions).
